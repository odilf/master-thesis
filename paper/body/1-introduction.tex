\chapter{Introduction}

In the Lagrangian formulation of mechanics, possible trajectories of a system in a configuration space ($q(t) \in Q$, where $Q$ is a smooth manifold) are given as the stationary points of the action, defined as $S(q(\cdot)) =\int L(q(t), \dot{q}(t)) \odif{t}$. While, of course, it is convenient to work analytically with these equations when possible; in practice, the Lagrangian is often either too complicated or fundamentally numerical-based. These cases are intractable to handle analytically, so it is very common to instead find approximations numerically.

In this paper we give an overview of discretization techniques used to find solutions to Lagrangian systems, namely the direct discretization of the so called ``Euler-Lagrange equations'', which preserve geometric properties of the underlying manifold of the system. We also showcase a state-of-the-art algorithm that can solve vector space Lagrangian systems in parallel with fixed boundary conditions~\cite{ferraroParallelIterativeMethod2025}.

Then, we provide a modest but novel contribution in extending this algorithm to work with forced systems and systems defined on Lie groups as configuration spaces. This makes the algorithm applicable to many more real-life classes of problems. For instance, forced systems give access to dissipation, nonholonomic constraints and externally controlled forces; and Lie groups to rigid body and multi-body dynamics, such as spacecraft attitude control and robotic manipulators.

While we do not provide a foundational and in-depth reference of mathematical and numerical computations of physical systems, this paper is meant to serve as a motivated introduction to numerical computations of Euler-Lagrange equations. For instance, a programmer with some mathematical background working on a satellite docking system would be able to implement a solver, but could also understand \emph{why} the algorithm works, and even why it has the structure it has in the first place. We believe that having this insight is valuable, especially as mechanical tasks keep getting more automated.

\paragraph{Objectives} The objectives of this thesis are:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item to review variational integration and the parallel iterative solver for the discrete Euler-Lagrange equations of~\cite{ferraroParallelIterativeMethod2025};
	\item to extend this solver to forced (Lagrange-d'Alembert) systems, together with provable convergence criteria; and
	\item to extend it to configuration spaces that are Lie groups, by trivializing to the Lie algebra, and providing convergence criteria.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Structure} The paper is structured as follows: In \cref{sec:background} we discuss mathematical tools and physical principles that frame the rest of the work. In \cref{sec:euler-lagrange-solutions} we explain how to approach discretization and to compute solutions of Lagrangian systems, showcasing specifically the parallel algorithm as presented in~\cite{ferraroParallelIterativeMethod2025}. Finally, we explain how to generalize said algorithm to forced systems in \cref{sec:forced-systems} and to Lie groups in \cref{sec:lie-groups}.
