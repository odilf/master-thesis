\begin{abstract}
\thispagestyle{plain}
\setcounter{page}{3}
	Variational integrators discretize the configuration space of a Lagrangian mechanical system rather than its equations of motion, making it possible to construct algorithms that preserve the geometric structure of the underlying manifold. Solving the resulting discrete Euler-Lagrange (DEL) equations is typically sequential, but a recent parallel Jacobi-Newton algorithm overcomes this, combining parallel Jacobi updates with local Newton-Raphson steps; its convergence is governed by a block-tridiagonal Hessian matrix. The algorithm is, however, formulated only for unforced systems on vector spaces.

	This thesis extends it in two directions, together with the corresponding convergence analysis. The first is to forced (Lagrange-d'Alembert) systems, such as those with dissipation; the method adapts by adding a force term, and converges if a per-edge condition holds, or if forces small enough relative to the contraction margin of the iteration are added to a convergent unforced system. The second is to configuration spaces that are Lie groups, such as those arising in rigid body dynamics; here the Newton-Raphson correction is computed in the Lie algebra and applied via a retraction and a pullback, and the convergence criteria, read in a Lie algebra chart at the solution where the retraction's curvature terms vanish, are structurally identical to the vector space case.
